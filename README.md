## Abstract

This research examines how Nigerian organisations across the banking, government,
and critical infrastructure sectors can build resilient IT infrastructure to prevent,
detect, respond to, and recover from cyberattacks.

Using a comparative case study approach, the research analyses the 2026
ByteToBreach campaign and its reported impact across Sterling Bank, Remita,
the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), and Ikeja Electric.

The analysis is guided by the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, IT Resilience
Theory, the Technology Acceptance Model, and the Shared Responsibility Model.

The research identifies technical, organisational, regulatory, and structural
factors contributing to cybersecurity resilience gaps and provides
sector-specific and cross-sector recommendations for strengthening Nigeria's
cybersecurity posture.

---

## Research Question

How can Nigerian organisations across banking, government, and critical
infrastructure sectors build resilient IT infrastructure to prevent, detect,
and respond to cyberattacks?

---

## Research Objectives

- Conduct a root cause analysis of IT infrastructure weaknesses across banking,
  government, and critical infrastructure sectors.
- Compare cybersecurity frameworks, including the NIST Cybersecurity Framework
  and the CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework.
- Identify cybersecurity resilience gaps across the sectors examined.
- Provide actionable recommendations for banks, fintech platforms, government
  agencies, and critical infrastructure stakeholders.

## Key Areas of Analysis

The research examines cybersecurity resilience across the five NIST CSF functions:

- Identify
- Protect
- Detect
- Respond
- Recover

It also analyses root causes across four dimensions:

- Technical
- Organisational
- Regulatory
- Structural

---

## Key Findings

The research identifies recurring resilience gaps across the cases examined,
particularly around:

- Vulnerability and patch management
- Credential and secrets management
- Third-party and supply-chain risk
- Security monitoring and detection
- Incident response and notification
- IT/OT network segmentation
- Cybersecurity governance and accountability
- Translation of cybersecurity policies into operational controls

The study argues that strengthening technical controls alone is insufficient
and highlights the importance of governance, accountability, risk management,
and cross-sector coordination.

---

## Recommendations

The research proposes recommendations for:

### Government and Regulators
- Strengthening national cyber incident response coordination
- Establishing sector-specific cybersecurity requirements
- Improving independent cybersecurity assessments
- Enhancing cross-sector threat intelligence sharing

### Banks and Fintech Platforms
- Formal vulnerability management programmes
- Zero-trust principles for third-party integrations
- Secure secrets and credential management
- Continuous security monitoring and SOC capabilities
- Stronger third-party security assessments

### Government Agencies
- Security-by-design requirements
- Executive cybersecurity awareness
- Dedicated cybersecurity funding
- Tested incident response and notification procedures

### Cross-Sector
- Shared security standards for public-private integrations
- National IT resilience assessments
- Cross-sector cybersecurity training
- Stronger collaboration between regulators and critical infrastructure operators

---

## Important Research Limitation

This research relies primarily on publicly available information.

Statements attributed to threat actors are treated as claims and are not
presented as independently verified facts unless supported by additional
evidence.

The limited availability of internal incident-response records also restricts
the depth of analysis possible for certain organisations and NIST CSF functions.
